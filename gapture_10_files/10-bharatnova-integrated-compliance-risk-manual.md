## Preface

BharatNova Financial Markets Ltd. ("BharatNova" or "the Company") is a diversified financial services organization offering securities broking, investment advisory and distribution, domestic and foreign remittance-related services, wealth management, and online customer onboarding. This Manual consolidates the Company's principal compliance and risk management requirements across these business lines into a single reference document. Individual business units maintain detailed standard operating procedures that operationalize the requirements set out below; where a conflict arises between this Manual and a business unit SOP, the matter shall be referred to Compliance for resolution.

This Manual reflects the Company's internal control design as understood by the Compliance & Risk function at the time of issuance, having regard to prevailing regulatory requirements applicable to BharatNova's licensed activities. It is not represented as an exhaustive restatement of every applicable regulatory obligation.

## Section 1. Customer Onboarding

1.1 Prospective customers shall complete the onboarding process through a branch, relationship manager, or the Company's digital onboarding platform, as described in Section 10 (Non-Resident and Digital Onboarding Considerations) and Section 2 (KYC/CDD) below.

1.2 No trading, investment, or remittance account shall be activated until onboarding documentation has been reviewed and the customer's risk classification (Section 3) has been assigned.

1.3 Onboarding staff shall verify that the customer's stated purpose for the relationship is consistent with the products and services requested.

1.4 Provisional activation of an account for a limited scope of services (e.g., view-only access) may be granted while outstanding documentation is being completed, at the discretion of the onboarding supervisor, provided full activation for transactional capability awaits completion of onboarding under Section 1.2.

## Section 2. KYC/CDD

2.1 Customer identity and address shall be verified using Officially Valid Documents or equivalent electronic verification mechanisms, consistent with the Company's KYC/CDD procedure.

2.2 KYC information shall be periodically reviewed based on the customer's risk classification under Section 3, subject to the requirements specified in the KYC/CDD procedure.

2.3 Where a customer holds accounts across more than one BharatNova business line (e.g., broking and wealth management), KYC completed for one business line may be relied upon for onboarding to another business line without independent re-verification, provided the KYC is current as defined in Section 2.2.

2.4 Enhanced due diligence shall apply to customers classified as High risk under Section 3, including verification of source of funds and senior management sign-off.

## Section 3. Risk Classification

3.1 Every customer shall be classified as Low, Medium, or High risk at onboarding, based on customer type, geography, product usage, transaction profile, and PEP status, and reclassified periodically or upon a material change in customer behavior.

3.2 Risk classification shall drive the applicable due diligence intensity (Section 2), transaction monitoring sensitivity (Section 6), and periodic review frequency across the customer lifecycle.

3.3 Risk classification overrides (e.g., downgrading a system-suggested High risk rating) may be approved by a Compliance Officer where supported by documented rationale, and shall be logged for periodic review as described in Section 26 (Compliance Monitoring).

## Section 4. Beneficial Ownership

4.1 For non-individual customers, beneficial owners holding a controlling interest, directly or indirectly, shall be identified and verified at onboarding, consistent with Section 2.

4.2 Where beneficial ownership cannot practicably be traced to the natural person level due to structural complexity, the senior managing official of the entity may be recorded in lieu, subject to Compliance concurrence.

4.3 Beneficial ownership records collected solely to support a specific onboarding decision, and not otherwise forming part of the customer's core KYC file, may be deleted once the onboarding decision has been finalized and is no longer under review, notwithstanding the general five-year retention standard described in Section 18.

## Section 5. Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

5.1 BharatNova maintains an AML program overseen by the Money Laundering Reporting Officer ("MLRO"), covering customer risk assessment, transaction monitoring, escalation, and regulatory reporting, as further detailed in the Company's standalone AML Policy.

5.2 All employees shall complete AML training as described in Section 28 (Employee Training).

5.3 The AML program's design and operating effectiveness shall be reviewed by Internal Audit as described in Section 25.

## Section 6. Transaction Monitoring

6.1 Customer transactions across all business lines shall be subject to monitoring calibrated to the customer's risk classification (Section 3), using a combination of automated alerts and periodic manual sampling.

6.2 Monitoring rules shall be reviewed periodically for continued relevance, subject to the requirements specified in the AML Policy.

6.3 Alerts shall be reviewed and dispositioned within a reasonable period, prioritized by severity, consistent with the alert framework maintained by the AML function.

## Section 7. Suspicious Activity Escalation

7.1 Any employee identifying activity suspected of being linked to money laundering, terrorist financing, or fraud shall escalate the matter immediately to the MLRO or the Compliance Officer, without prior notice to the customer concerned.

7.2 The MLRO shall determine whether grounds exist for filing a suspicious transaction report, and shall proceed with such filing within the timelines prescribed under applicable regulatory requirements.

7.3 Escalations under Section 7.1 shall not be delayed pending completion of any parallel operational process (such as a pending transaction or account review) that may be affected by the escalation.

## Section 8. Remittance Processing

8.1 Domestic and cross-border remittance instructions shall be processed in accordance with the Company's Remittance Operations procedure, incorporating customer identification, transaction approval (Section 11), and beneficiary verification (Section 10) requirements.

8.2 Remittance transactions above the threshold specified in the Remittance Operations procedure shall require independent maker-checker approval prior to release of funds, as described in Section 11.

8.3 Where the designated checker is unavailable and the customer's remittance is time-sensitive (for example, a same-day payment deadline), the branch manager may release the transaction under a self-approval exception, with independent checking completed retrospectively on a best-effort basis, as further described in Section 11.3.

## Section 9. Foreign Remittance

9.1 Cross-border remittances shall be subject to purpose classification, supporting documentation, and screening requirements consistent with the Company's Foreign Remittance procedure and applicable foreign exchange management requirements.

9.2 Outward remittances exceeding the threshold specified in the Foreign Remittance procedure shall require Compliance Officer sign-off prior to release of funds.

9.3 Where the remitting or correspondent bank has already performed screening and purpose validation as part of its own processing, BharatNova's operational checks may rely on the correspondent's confirmation without independent re-verification, unless the transaction is otherwise flagged under Section 6.

## Section 10. Beneficiary Verification

10.1 Beneficiary account details shall be independently verified — through a penny-drop mechanism, bank confirmation letter, or equivalent — prior to the first remittance to a new beneficiary, across both domestic and foreign remittance channels.

10.2 Subsequent changes to a previously verified beneficiary's account details shall likewise require independent verification prior to the next remittance to that beneficiary, except as provided in Section 10.3.

10.3 Beneficiary change requests submitted via the customer's registered email, accompanied by the customer ID and previous beneficiary reference, may be actioned by the processing team upon receipt, with a confirmation notification sent to the customer's registered contact details after the change; this channel is available as an operational convenience for established customers with a transaction history of good standing, notwithstanding the independent verification standard described in Section 10.1–10.2.

## Section 11. Transaction Approval

11.1 Transactions across remittance, trading, and wealth management channels shall be subject to maker-checker approval commensurate with transaction value and risk, with the checker independent of the maker.

11.2 Approval thresholds and workflows are set out in the relevant business unit procedure (Remittance Operations, Securities Trading, or Wealth Management, as applicable).

11.3 Emergency self-approval by a supervisor, in lieu of independent checking, may be applied in the circumstances described in Section 8.3, subject to retrospective review by Compliance and logging in the exception register described in Section 29.

## Section 12. Segregation of Duties

12.1 The functions of transaction initiation, approval, reconciliation, and record maintenance shall, as far as practicable, be performed by different individuals across all business lines.

12.2 At smaller branch locations where staffing constraints exist, a single employee may perform more than one of the functions described in Section 12.1, subject to a periodic post-facto sample review by Compliance, the frequency of which is determined based on the branch's transaction volume and risk profile.

## Section 13. Securities Trading

13.1 Client orders shall be received, authenticated, recorded, and executed in accordance with the Company's Securities Trading and Order Management procedure, including individual dealer authentication as the default access model.

13.2 Dealer terminal access shall be provisioned through individually assigned credentials; temporary supervisor-authorized access arrangements during dealer unavailability shall be logged as described in the Securities Trading procedure.

13.3 Surveillance shall monitor trading activity for patterns indicative of unauthorized trading, front-running, or market manipulation, and shall escalate findings to Compliance consistent with Section 7 and Section 26.

## Section 14. Client Funds

14.1 Client funds shall be maintained in segregated client bank accounts, distinct from BharatNova's proprietary funds, and shall not be used to meet the Company's own obligations.

14.2 Client fund reconciliation shall be performed periodically by a function independent of trade execution, consistent with the segregation of duties principle in Section 12.

## Section 15. Investor Protection

15.1 Client securities shall be held in a manner that preserves client ownership rights and shall not be commingled with the Company's proprietary holdings, except as permitted under an explicit client authorization (e.g., margin pledge).

15.2 Investor protection measures, including risk disclosures and suitability assessments for wealth management products, shall be documented at the point of sale.

## Section 16. Complaint Handling

16.1 Investor and customer complaints shall be logged in the centralized Complaint Management System, classified by severity, and routed to the responsible business unit for resolution, consistent with the Company's Investor Grievance Redressal procedure.

16.2 Complaints involving suspected unauthorized transactions shall be escalated immediately to Compliance and Surveillance, consistent with the immediate escalation standard described in Section 7.1.

16.3 Complaints not involving suspected unauthorized activity shall be escalated to the next level of authority if unresolved within three business days beyond the applicable service level, as set out in the Investor Grievance Redressal procedure's escalation table.

## Section 17. Regulatory Reporting

17.1 BharatNova shall submit periodic and event-based regulatory reports across its business lines — including AML/STR filings, cross-border transaction reports, trading and risk returns, and cybersecurity incident notifications — within the timelines prescribed under applicable regulatory requirements.

17.2 Where the full information required for a report is not available by the applicable deadline due to data reconciliation or investigation timelines, an initial submission may be made on a best-available-information basis, with supplementary filings made as further information becomes available, consistent with the approach described in the relevant business unit procedure.

17.3 Compliance shall maintain a consolidated regulatory reporting calendar covering all business lines, reviewed periodically for completeness against known regulatory requirements.

## Section 18. Record Retention

18.1 Customer, transaction, KYC, and remittance records shall be retained for a minimum of five years from the date of the transaction or termination of the customer relationship, whichever is later, or such longer period as required under a business-line-specific standard (for example, the eight-year standard applicable to remittance transaction records under the Remittance Operations procedure) or an applicable legal hold.

18.2 Notwithstanding Section 18.1, records that are collected solely to support an internal operational decision and do not form part of the customer's core transaction or KYC file — such as certain internal beneficial ownership working papers described in Section 4.3, or internal vendor correspondence described in Section 22 — may be deleted once the underlying business purpose has been fulfilled, subject to confirmation that no legal hold applies.

18.3 Departments shall apply the retention period appropriate to the specific record category in their custody, having regard to the differing standards referenced across this Manual and underlying business unit procedures.

## Section 19. Audit Trails

19.1 Core systems supporting transaction processing, trading, remittance, and regulatory reporting shall maintain audit trails capturing record creation, modification, and deletion events, together with the identity of the acting user and timestamp, designed to be tamper-evident.

19.2 System administrators retain the technical capability to access and, where necessary for system maintenance or error correction, modify or annotate log entries in certain business-line systems, as further described in Section 20 (Cybersecurity) and the Information Security Policy; such administrative actions are captured in a secondary system change log reviewed as part of periodic IT audit activity rather than in real time.

19.3 Audit trail records shall be retained consistent with the retention period applicable to the underlying record, as described in Section 18.

## Section 20. Cybersecurity

20.1 Access to information systems shall be provisioned on a least-privilege, role-based basis, with privileged administrator accounts subject to the additional controls described in the Information Security Policy.

20.2 Significant cybersecurity incidents shall be escalated internally to the CISO and reported to regulatory authorities within the timelines prescribed under applicable requirements; internal escalation of a suspected significant incident to the CISO should occur within three business days of initial detection by the reporting team, to allow for preliminary triage before formal classification, distinct from the immediate escalation standard applied to suspected suspicious transactions under Section 7.1.

20.3 Backup and disaster recovery arrangements are addressed in Section 24 (Business Continuity).

## Section 21. Access Control

21.1 Access to customer data, trading systems, and remittance processing systems shall be granted based on job role and reviewed periodically for continued appropriateness.

21.2 Emergency access provisions, including shared emergency credentials for after-hours critical system support, are maintained under a controlled process (sealed credential access, logged usage, next-business-day reporting to Information Security) as described in the Information Security Policy.

21.3 Access logs form part of the audit trail framework described in Section 19 and are subject to the same administrator-modification considerations noted in Section 19.2.

## Section 22. Outsourcing

22.1 Engagement of outsourced service providers for functions material to BharatNova's regulated activities shall follow the due diligence, risk classification, and agreement requirements set out in the Outsourcing and Vendor Management procedure.

22.2 Outsourcing agreements for Critical and High-risk vendors shall include audit rights and, where applicable under prevailing regulatory requirements, provisions enabling regulatory access to vendor premises and records relevant to the outsourced activity.

22.3 Urgent engagement of a replacement vendor to address an operational disruption may proceed under a provisional onboarding exception, with full due diligence completed within a reasonable period thereafter, consistent with the emergency onboarding provision described in the Outsourcing and Vendor Management procedure.

## Section 23. Vendor Management

23.1 Vendor performance, incident history, and continued risk classification shall be reviewed at the frequency applicable to the vendor's risk tier, as set out in the Outsourcing and Vendor Management procedure.

23.2 Vendors with access to customer data shall be contractually obligated to notify BharatNova of any security incident affecting the services provided; BharatNova's own incident escalation and reporting timelines under Section 20.2 begin to run from the point of vendor notification.

23.3 Upon termination of a vendor relationship, the vendor shall return or securely delete BharatNova data in its possession and certify completion of this process, subject to the record retention considerations described in Section 18.2 regarding related internal working papers.

## Section 24. Business Continuity

24.1 BharatNova shall maintain business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements for systems and functions classified as critical, including periodic testing of backup restoration and failover capability.

24.2 Business continuity planning shall account for the potential unavailability of Critical vendors identified under Section 22–23, including identification of alternate arrangements where feasible.

24.3 Testing frequency for lower-criticality systems may, in practice, be deferred where competing priorities arise, with testing completed in a subsequent cycle, consistent with the approach described in the Information Security Policy.

## Section 25. Internal Audit

25.1 Internal Audit shall maintain a risk-based audit plan covering all business lines and control areas described in this Manual, including AML, KYC, trading operations, remittance operations, cybersecurity, outsourcing, and record retention.

25.2 Audit findings shall be reported to the Board Audit Committee together with management's remediation plan and target closure dates; repeat findings across consecutive cycles shall be escalated for direct Board oversight of remediation.

## Section 26. Compliance Monitoring

26.1 The Compliance function shall conduct periodic monitoring across business lines, including sample-based review of onboarding files, transaction approvals, exception registers, and complaint handling, consistent with the monitoring provisions described in the relevant business unit procedures.

26.2 Compliance monitoring findings indicating a pattern of reliance on exception processes (such as the emergency self-approval provision in Section 8.3 and Section 11.3, or the beneficiary email-change channel in Section 10.3) shall be reported to senior management, with consideration given to whether the underlying exception process requires tightening.

## Section 27. Regulatory Change Management

27.1 Compliance shall monitor developments in applicable regulatory requirements relevant to BharatNova's licensed activities and shall assess the impact of material changes on this Manual and underlying business unit procedures.

27.2 Updates arising from regulatory change shall be incorporated into this Manual and communicated to affected business units within a reasonable period following the change.

## Section 28. Employee Training

28.1 All employees shall complete induction training covering the principal requirements of this Manual, including AML, KYC, information security, and code of conduct, and shall complete refresher training on an annual basis.

28.2 Role-specific advanced training (for dealers, AML analysts, and Compliance staff) shall be provided at a frequency determined by the relevant function head based on training resource availability and regulatory developments.

## Section 29. Policy Exceptions

29.1 Exceptions to the requirements described in this Manual — including the emergency self-approval provisions described in Sections 8.3 and 11.3, and provisional onboarding exceptions described in Sections 1.4 and 22.3 — shall be logged in the relevant business unit's exception register and reviewed by Compliance as part of periodic monitoring under Section 26.

29.2 Exceptions involving High-risk customers, Critical vendors, or matters otherwise escalated under Section 7 shall additionally require Compliance Officer or MLRO approval, as applicable, and shall be reported to senior management.

## Section 30. Management Oversight

30.1 Senior management shall receive periodic consolidated reporting covering AML and transaction monitoring metrics, complaint volumes and ageing, exception register activity, cybersecurity incidents, outsourcing risk, and internal audit findings, as described across the relevant sections of this Manual.

30.2 The Board shall review this Manual, together with the underlying business unit procedures it references, at least annually, or earlier where triggered by a material regulatory development, significant incident, or internal audit finding, and shall approve any material amendments.

30.3 Nothing in this Manual shall be construed as superseding a more stringent requirement set out in an applicable regulatory circular, master direction, or statute; where such a conflict is identified, the business unit shall refer the matter to Compliance for resolution and interim guidance pending formal amendment of this Manual.
