## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Policy establishes Vertex Wealth & Securities Ltd.'s ("Vertex" or "the Company") framework for the prevention, detection, escalation, and reporting of money laundering and terrorist financing risk arising from its securities and wealth management activities. It applies to all customer relationships, transactions, and employees, and shall be read together with the Company's KYC Policy and Sanctions Screening Procedures.

## 2. AML Governance

**AML-01.** The Board shall approve this Policy and shall review its effectiveness at least annually.

**AML-02.** The Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) shall be a senior officer designated by the Board, responsible for the overall AML program, including policy, monitoring, investigation, and regulatory reporting.

**AML-03.** An AML Committee, comprising the MLRO, Head of Operations, and Head of Risk, shall meet quarterly to review AML program metrics, escalations, and emerging typologies.

**AML-04.** The MLRO shall have unrestricted access to all customer and transaction data necessary to perform AML functions, and shall have a reporting line to the Board that is independent of business line management.

**AML-05.** Business heads shall be responsible for ensuring AML controls are embedded in day-to-day operations within their function; where business targets and AML control requirements appear to conflict, the business head may seek guidance from the MLRO, though no formal escalation timeline is specified for such conflicts.

## 3. Customer Risk Assessment

**AML-06.** Every customer shall be assigned an AML risk rating at onboarding based on customer type, geography, product usage, and transaction profile.

**AML-07.** Risk ratings shall be re-assessed at the frequency specified in the KYC Policy, and additionally whenever a significant change in customer behavior is observed.

**AML-08.** Customer risk assessment methodology shall be documented and approved by the MLRO, and reviewed periodically for continued relevance.

### Table 1: Customer Risk Categories

| Risk Category | Indicative Criteria | Monitoring Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Salaried individuals, domestic, transparent source of funds | Standard periodic monitoring |
| Medium | Self-employed, SMEs, moderate cash usage | Enhanced periodic monitoring |
| High | PEPs, complex structures, high-risk jurisdiction exposure, cash-intensive business | Continuous monitoring with quarterly review |

## 4. Transaction Monitoring

**AML-09.** All customer transactions shall be subject to automated monitoring using a rules-based transaction monitoring system, supplemented by periodic manual sampling.

**AML-10.** Monitoring rules shall be calibrated based on customer risk category, product type, and historical alert quality, and shall be reviewed at least annually by the AML Committee.

**AML-11.** The transaction monitoring system shall generate alerts for review by the AML Operations team on a daily basis.

**AML-12.** Where system latency or data feed issues delay alert generation, the AML Operations team shall process the backlog within a reasonable period once normal operations resume; a specific maximum backlog clearance period is not defined in this Policy.

## 5. Unusual Transaction Identification

**AML-13.** Transactions inconsistent with a customer's declared profile, occupation, or historical pattern shall be flagged for review.

**AML-14.** Indicators of unusual activity include, without limitation: rapid movement of funds shortly after deposit, transactions just below reporting thresholds, use of multiple accounts for a single beneficial owner, and transactions involving counterparties in high-risk jurisdictions.

**AML-15.** Front-line staff shall be trained to recognize behavioral red flags at the point of customer interaction and to refer such observations to AML Operations.

## 6. Suspicious Activity Escalation

**AML-16.** Alerts assessed as warranting further review shall be escalated to a Level 2 analyst within the AML Operations team.

**AML-17.** Level 2 analysts shall complete their assessment and recommend disposition — close, monitor, or escalate for investigation — within a reasonable time frame commensurate with alert severity.

**AML-18.** Escalations to the MLRO for potential suspicious transaction reporting shall be made promptly upon a Level 2 analyst's determination that grounds for suspicion exist; "promptly" in this context is understood by AML Operations to mean within the same review cycle, generally not exceeding a few business days depending on case complexity.

## 7. Alert Generation

**AML-19.** Alert types shall include, at a minimum: threshold breaches, structuring patterns, dormant account reactivation, high-risk jurisdiction counterparties, and PEP-linked transactions.

**AML-20.** Each alert shall be assigned a severity rating at the point of generation, which shall inform prioritization of analyst review.

### Table 2: Alert Severity Classification

| Severity | Description | Target Review Timeline | Ownership |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | Sanctions/PEP match, suspected terrorist financing indicators | Same business day | MLRO / Senior Analyst |
| High | Structuring pattern, high-risk jurisdiction, large unexplained transfer | Within 3 business days | Level 2 Analyst |
| Medium | Threshold breach without other risk indicators | Within 5 business days | Level 1 Analyst |
| Low | Minor deviation from expected pattern | Within next monthly cycle | Level 1 Analyst |

**AML-21.** Analysts may reprioritize alert severity based on additional context obtained during review, subject to supervisory concurrence.

## 8. Transaction Thresholds

**AML-22.** Cash transactions above the threshold specified in the Company's Cash Transaction Reporting procedure shall be automatically flagged for review and reporting consideration.

**AML-23.** Aggregated transaction thresholds shall be monitored on a rolling basis to identify potential structuring; however, the look-back window applied by the monitoring system may vary by product line based on system configuration constraints.

## 9. Structuring Detection

**AML-24.** The monitoring system shall include rules designed to detect structuring, including multiple transactions by a single customer or linked customers that individually fall below reporting thresholds but in aggregate exceed them.

**AML-25.** Structuring alerts shall be treated as at least High severity and escalated accordingly.

**AML-26.** Where structuring is suspected but the individual transactions each have a plausible independent business rationale, the reviewing analyst has discretion to close the alert with a documented explanation, without further escalation.

## 10. High-Risk Jurisdictions

**AML-27.** The Company shall maintain a list of high-risk jurisdictions, informed by internationally recognized sources, and shall apply enhanced monitoring to transactions involving counterparties in such jurisdictions.

**AML-28.** The high-risk jurisdiction list shall be reviewed and updated periodically by the MLRO's team.

**AML-29.** Transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions below a certain value may be processed without additional review where the customer is otherwise classified as low-risk, at the discretion of the AML Operations team.

## 11. Customer Profiling

**AML-30.** A behavioral profile shall be maintained for each customer, incorporating expected transaction volume, frequency, and typical counterparties, to support anomaly detection.

**AML-31.** Profiles shall be updated periodically based on observed transaction activity; significant deviations from the profile shall trigger a review of the underlying risk rating.

## 12. Investigation Workflow

**AML-32.** Cases escalated for investigation shall be assigned to an AML Investigator, who shall gather relevant transaction history, KYC information, and any available external information.

**AML-33.** Investigation findings shall be documented in a case file and presented to the MLRO for a filing decision.

### Table 3: Investigation Ownership and Escalation

| Stage | Owner | Escalates To | Typical Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial alert review | Level 1 Analyst | Level 2 Analyst | 1–5 business days |
| Case assessment | Level 2 Analyst | AML Investigator | 3–7 business days |
| Formal investigation | AML Investigator | MLRO | Case-dependent |
| Filing decision | MLRO | Board AML Committee (summary) | Per regulatory timeline |

**AML-34.** Investigations involving senior management, large institutional clients, or connected parties of the Company shall be escalated directly to the MLRO and, where warranted, to the Board AML Committee.

## 13. Compliance Officer Review

**AML-35.** The MLRO shall review a sample of closed alerts each month to assess analyst decision quality and identify training needs.

**AML-36.** The MLRO shall periodically certify to the Board AML Committee that the transaction monitoring program is operating as designed.

## 14. Regulatory Reporting

**AML-37.** The MLRO shall be responsible for filing Suspicious Transaction Reports and other regulatory reports with the Financial Intelligence Unit and other applicable authorities.

**AML-38.** Reports shall be filed within the timelines prescribed by applicable regulatory requirements. Where a filing decision is complex or requires additional legal input, the MLRO may take additional time to finalize the filing beyond the initial internal target, provided the delay is documented and the matter remains under active review.

**AML-39.** No employee shall disclose to a customer, directly or indirectly, that a suspicious transaction report has been filed or is being considered.

## 15. Record Keeping

**AML-40.** All AML-related records, including alerts, investigation files, and filed reports, shall be retained for a minimum of five years, or longer where required by ongoing legal or regulatory proceedings.

**AML-41.** Records shall be maintained in a manner that permits reconstruction of the investigation timeline and rationale for the filing decision.

## 16. Employee Training

**AML-42.** All employees shall complete AML training at induction and annually thereafter, covering red flags, reporting obligations, and consequences of non-compliance.

**AML-43.** Employees in customer-facing and AML Operations roles shall receive role-specific advanced training, the frequency and content of which shall be determined by the MLRO's team based on available training resources and scheduling.

**AML-44.** Training completion shall be tracked, and non-completion escalated to the relevant department head.

## 17. Internal Audit

**AML-45.** Internal Audit shall include the AML program within its audit universe and shall assess the design and operating effectiveness of controls described in this Policy.

**AML-46.** Audit findings shall be reported to the Board AML Committee along with management's remediation plan and target closure dates.

**AML-47.** Repeat findings from consecutive audit cycles shall be escalated to the Board for direct oversight of remediation.

## 18. Miscellaneous Controls

**AML-48.** This Policy shall take precedence over conflicting departmental procedures; where a conflict is identified, it shall be referred to the MLRO for resolution.

**AML-49.** Exceptions to this Policy shall be documented and approved by the MLRO or, in the MLRO's absence, by the Head of Risk, and reported to the AML Committee at its next meeting.

**AML-50.** This Policy shall be reviewed at least annually and updated to reflect changes in regulatory expectations, business activities, and emerging money laundering typologies.
