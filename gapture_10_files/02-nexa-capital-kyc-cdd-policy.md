## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Policy sets out the requirements for customer identification, Know Your Customer ("KYC"), and Customer Due Diligence ("CDD") applicable to all clients of Nexa Capital Markets Pvt. Ltd. ("Nexa" or "the Company"), including individuals, non-individuals, and institutional clients availing securities and investment services. It applies to onboarding, ongoing monitoring, and periodic review activities across all business lines.

## 2. Definitions

- **CDD**: The process of identifying a customer and verifying that identity, along with understanding the nature of the customer's business relationship.
- **EDD**: Enhanced Due Diligence, applied to higher-risk customer categories.
- **PEP**: Politically Exposed Person.
- **Beneficial Owner**: The natural person(s) who ultimately own or control a customer, or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted.

## 3. Customer Onboarding

3.1 No account shall be opened or activated for trading or investment services until the KYC process, as set out in this Policy, has been completed to the satisfaction of the onboarding team.

3.2 Onboarding staff shall make reasonable efforts to obtain complete and accurate customer information, including identity documents, address proof, financial background, and the purpose of the account relationship.

3.3 Incomplete applications may be provisionally accepted at the discretion of the branch head, with the customer given a follow-up period to submit outstanding documents before the account is activated for trading.

## 4. Identity Verification

4.1 Identity of individual customers shall be verified using an Officially Valid Document (OVD) as prescribed under applicable regulatory guidance.

4.2 For non-individual customers, identity verification shall extend to the constitution documents of the entity (certificate of incorporation, partnership deed, trust deed, or equivalent), along with authorized signatory details.

4.3 Physical verification of original documents shall be carried out wherever the customer visits a branch in person. For documents submitted through digital channels, the onboarding system shall perform automated validation checks where technically available.

## 5. PAN Validation

5.1 A valid Permanent Account Number ("PAN") shall be obtained and validated against the database of the Income Tax Department (or an equivalent validation service) prior to account activation.

5.2 Where PAN validation through the online service is temporarily unavailable, onboarding staff may proceed with manual verification of the physical PAN card and complete the online validation retrospectively as soon as the service is restored.

## 6. KYC Records

6.1 KYC records shall include, at a minimum, proof of identity, proof of address, a recent photograph, financial and occupational details, and the customer's signature.

6.2 KYC records shall be uploaded to the centralized KYC Registration Agency (KRA) system in accordance with applicable timelines. Uploads shall generally be completed within a reasonable period following account activation; where system integration issues arise, uploads may be batched and processed in the following cycle.

## 7. Customer Risk Classification

7.1 Every customer shall be classified into a risk category — Low, Medium, or High — at the time of onboarding, based on factors including customer type, geography, nature of transactions, source of funds, and occupation.

7.2 The risk classification shall be recorded in the customer master and shall drive the applicable due diligence and periodic review frequency, as set out in Section 11.

### Table 1: Illustrative Risk Classification Criteria

| Factor | Low Risk | Medium Risk | High Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer type | Salaried individual | Self-employed / SME | Trusts, NGOs, complex structures |
| Geography | Domestic, non-sensitive | Domestic, high cash-intensity sector | Cross-border / high-risk jurisdiction |
| Transaction pattern | Consistent with profile | Occasionally inconsistent | Frequently inconsistent or unexplained |
| PEP status | No | No | Yes, or close associate of PEP |

## 8. Low, Medium, and High-Risk Customers

8.1 Low-risk customers shall be subject to standard due diligence at onboarding.

8.2 Medium-risk customers shall be subject to standard due diligence at onboarding and closer transaction monitoring thereafter.

8.3 High-risk customers shall be subject to Enhanced Due Diligence as described in Section 9, and shall require sign-off from a designated Compliance Officer prior to account activation.

## 9. Enhanced Due Diligence

9.1 EDD shall include, at a minimum, verification of source of funds, senior management approval for onboarding, and more frequent transaction monitoring.

9.2 For customers classified as high-risk based on occupation or geography alone (as opposed to adverse information), the onboarding team may apply a reduced EDD checklist, focused primarily on source-of-funds declaration, subject to Compliance concurrence.

## 10. Beneficial Ownership

10.1 For non-individual customers, the beneficial owner(s) holding, directly or indirectly, a controlling ownership interest shall be identified and verified.

10.2 Where the ownership structure is layered across multiple entities, onboarding staff shall make reasonable efforts to trace beneficial ownership to the natural person level; where this is not practicable due to the complexity of the structure or non-cooperation of the customer, the senior managing official of the entity may be recorded as the beneficial owner in lieu of the natural person.

## 11. Periodic KYC Review

11.1 KYC information shall be reviewed periodically based on the customer's risk classification: every two years for high-risk customers, every eight years for medium-risk customers, and every ten years for low-risk customers, or as otherwise required by applicable regulatory guidance.

11.2 Where a customer does not respond to a periodic KYC review request within a reasonable period, the relationship manager may escalate the matter for account restriction as described in Section 14, though no fixed escalation timeline is prescribed for this step.

11.3 Notwithstanding Section 11.1, updated identity and address proof obtained during any interim customer-initiated interaction (such as a nominee update or bank account change) may be treated as satisfying the periodic KYC review requirement for that cycle, regardless of whether all elements of a full periodic review were completed.

## 12. Customer Information Updates

12.1 Customers shall be encouraged to promptly inform Nexa of any change in address, contact details, or other material information.

12.2 Updates to customer information received through the customer portal shall be processed by Operations upon system validation; updates received via email shall be processed upon reasonable verification of the sender's identity, which may include confirmation of registered contact details already on file.

## 13. Suspicious Customer Behavior

13.1 Relationship managers and operations staff shall remain alert to behavior inconsistent with a customer's stated profile, including unexplained changes in trading pattern, reluctance to provide documentation, or use of multiple accounts to circumvent limits.

13.2 Suspected suspicious behavior shall be reported to the Compliance Officer, who shall determine the appropriate course of action, including potential filing of a suspicious transaction report.

## 14. Account Restrictions

14.1 Accounts with incomplete or overdue KYC shall be subject to restrictions on further transactions, in a manner and timeline to be determined by Operations in consultation with Compliance.

14.2 Restricted accounts may be reactivated upon completion of outstanding KYC requirements, subject to sign-off by the branch head.

## 15. Record Retention

15.1 KYC records and supporting documentation shall be retained for a minimum of five years following the termination of the customer relationship, or such longer period as required for ongoing legal or regulatory matters.

15.2 Records may be maintained in physical or electronic form, provided they remain retrievable and legible throughout the retention period.

## 16. Compliance Officer Responsibilities

16.1 The Compliance Officer shall be responsible for overall supervision of the KYC/CDD framework, approval of high-risk onboarding, and periodic reporting to the Compliance & Risk Committee.

16.2 The Compliance Officer shall have the authority to approve reasonable exceptions to this Policy on a case-by-case basis, provided such exceptions are documented.

## 17. Audit and Management Review

17.1 Internal Audit shall review the KYC/CDD framework as part of its audit plan, with the frequency to be determined based on the annual risk-based audit schedule.

17.2 Findings from KYC-related audits shall be reported to the Compliance & Risk Committee, along with a remediation plan where deficiencies are identified.

17.3 Management shall periodically review aggregate KYC compliance metrics, including the proportion of overdue periodic reviews and high-risk account onboarding volumes.

## 18. Policy Exceptions

18.1 Exceptions to this Policy that are not otherwise addressed in specific sections above may be approved by the relevant Operations Manager where the exception is operational in nature and does not involve a high-risk customer, provided the rationale is documented in the exception log.

18.2 Exceptions involving high-risk customers or beneficial ownership determinations shall require Compliance Officer approval.

## 19. Document Review

This Policy shall be reviewed periodically to reflect changes in the Company's business, risk appetite, and applicable regulatory expectations. The review shall be coordinated by the Compliance Officer.
