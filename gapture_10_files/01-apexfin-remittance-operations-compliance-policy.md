## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Policy establishes the framework governing domestic and cross-border remittance operations conducted by ApexFin Securities & Financial Services Pvt. Ltd. ("ApexFin" or "the Company"), including customer identification, transaction processing, sanctions screening, monitoring, reporting, and record-keeping. It applies to all employees, contractors, and outsourced service providers involved in remittance-related functions.

This Policy is intended to give effect to applicable regulatory expectations issued by the Reserve Bank of India and the Securities and Exchange Board of India from time to time, as well as internal risk management standards adopted by the Company. Where a specific regulatory circular or master direction prescribes a more stringent requirement than set out herein, the regulatory requirement shall prevail, and the relevant business unit is responsible for identifying such instances.

## 2. Definitions

- **Remittance**: Transfer of funds initiated by a customer, whether domestic or cross-border, processed through the Company's payment channels or correspondent banking arrangements.
- **Beneficiary**: The intended recipient of a remittance.
- **Maker-Checker**: A two-person control whereby one employee initiates a transaction ("maker") and a second, independent employee approves it ("checker") before execution.
- **Sanctions Screening**: The process of checking customer and beneficiary details against applicable sanctions and watch lists.
- **STR/CTR**: Suspicious Transaction Report / Cash Transaction Report, as applicable.

## 3. Governance and Ownership

The Head of Remittance Operations & Compliance shall own this Policy and shall be responsible for its periodic review, updates, and dissemination. The Compliance Department shall maintain oversight of adherence and shall report material breaches to the Board Risk & Compliance Committee on a quarterly basis, or sooner where the breach is assessed as significant.

## 4. Customer Identification for Remittance Services

4.1 Every customer initiating a remittance shall be identified using a valid government-issued identity document and address proof prior to onboarding, in accordance with the Company's KYC Policy.

4.2 Walk-in or one-time remittance customers below the prescribed threshold may be onboarded through a simplified identification process, subject to the transaction limits specified in Annexure A.

4.3 Where a customer's identity cannot be independently verified through electronic means, the branch manager may, at their discretion, accept manual verification supported by two independent documents.

## 5. KYC and Customer Due Diligence for Remittance Transactions

5.1 KYC shall be completed for all customers prior to the first remittance transaction, and updated periodically based on the customer's risk classification.

5.2 For existing account holders of the Company who have previously completed KYC for other product lines, Remittance Operations may rely on the KYC already on file and is not required to independently re-verify the customer's identity for remittance purposes, unless the customer's risk category is High or the KYC is more than three years old.

5.3 Enhanced due diligence shall be applied to customers classified as high-risk, including politically exposed persons, in accordance with the Company's Customer Risk Classification framework.

5.4 Where customer information appears inconsistent with the stated purpose or value of a remittance, staff should exercise judgment in determining whether additional documentation is warranted.

## 6. Domestic Remittance Processing

6.1 Domestic remittances shall be processed through empanelled banking partners and payment system operators authorized to handle such transactions.

6.2 All domestic remittance instructions shall be recorded in the core remittance system with a unique transaction reference number, time stamp, and originating employee ID.

6.3 Same-day processing shall be attempted for all domestic remittances received before the daily cut-off time; remittances received after cut-off shall normally be processed on the next business day.

## 7. Cross-Border Remittance Processing

7.1 Cross-border remittances shall be routed only through correspondent banks and payment partners that have been subjected to due diligence by the Compliance Department.

7.2 Purpose codes for all cross-border remittances shall be captured and validated against supporting documentation (invoices, purchase orders, remittance applications, or equivalent).

7.3 Cross-border remittances exceeding the threshold specified in Annexure A shall require additional supporting documentation and sign-off from a designated Compliance Officer.

7.4 Where supporting documentation is incomplete but the customer is a long-standing relationship of good standing, the processing officer may proceed with the transaction and request the missing documentation within a reasonable period thereafter.

## 8. Beneficiary Verification and Beneficiary Change Management

8.1 Beneficiary bank account details shall be verified at the time of first remittance to a new beneficiary through a penny-drop or equivalent verification mechanism, where technically feasible.

8.2 Subsequent remittances to a previously verified beneficiary shall not require re-verification unless the beneficiary account details have changed.

8.3 Requests to change beneficiary account details may be accepted via the customer's registered email address, provided the request references the existing customer ID and the previous beneficiary details. Telephonic confirmation with the customer is recommended but not mandatory where the customer has a transaction history of more than twelve months with the Company.

8.4 Beneficiary changes shall be logged in the system; however, urgent changes required to meet a customer's time-sensitive remittance may be actioned by the processing officer ahead of supervisory confirmation, with supervisory sign-off obtained within the same business day on a best-effort basis.

## 9. Transaction Approval and Maker-Checker Controls

9.1 All remittance transactions above the retail threshold specified in Annexure A shall be subject to maker-checker approval, with the checker being an employee independent of the maker.

9.2 Transactions below the retail threshold may be processed by a single authorized employee without independent checking, subject to system-based transaction limit controls.

9.3 In circumstances where the designated checker is unavailable — including outside business hours, during system outages, or where urgent customer service commitments require same-day processing — the branch manager or shift supervisor may approve the transaction as an emergency single-authorization exception. All such exceptions shall be recorded in an exception register and reviewed by Compliance during the next monthly review cycle.

9.4 Emergency exceptions under Section 9.3 shall not, in principle, exceed 5% of monthly transaction volume at any branch; this threshold serves as a guidance benchmark rather than a hard system-enforced limit.

## 10. Sanctions Screening

10.1 All customers and beneficiaries shall be screened against applicable sanctions lists, including UN, OFAC, and domestic watch lists, prior to transaction execution.

10.2 Positive or potential matches shall be escalated to the Compliance Department and the transaction placed on hold pending resolution.

10.3 Screening shall be performed using the Company's approved screening tool; fuzzy-match thresholds shall be configured by IT in consultation with Compliance and reviewed annually.

## 11. Transaction Monitoring

11.1 All remittance transactions shall be subject to post-facto monitoring by the Compliance Department using rule-based alerts configured in the transaction monitoring system.

11.2 Alerts generated by the monitoring system shall be reviewed and dispositioned within a reasonable timeframe, factoring in analyst workload and alert volume.

11.3 Patterns indicative of structuring (i.e., splitting transactions to avoid thresholds) shall be escalated for investigation where identified.

## 12. Regulatory Reporting

12.1 The Compliance Department shall be responsible for filing all applicable regulatory reports, including suspicious transaction reports and cross-border transaction reports, with the relevant authorities.

12.2 Reports shall generally be filed within the timelines prescribed under applicable regulatory requirements. Where operational constraints, system downtime, or data quality issues delay report preparation, the Compliance Officer may extend internal preparation timelines as deemed necessary, provided the delay is documented.

12.3 A summary of regulatory filings shall be presented to the Board Risk & Compliance Committee periodically.

## 13. Record Retention

13.1 Remittance transaction records, KYC documentation, and supporting evidence shall be retained for a minimum of five years from the date of the transaction or the termination of the customer relationship, whichever is later, or such longer period as may be required for ongoing legal or regulatory proceedings.

13.2 Records may be retained in electronic form, provided such records are retrievable in a legible format upon request.

## 14. Audit Trails

14.1 The remittance processing system shall maintain a complete audit trail of all transaction actions, including creation, approval, modification, and cancellation, along with the identity of the acting employee and timestamp.

14.2 System administrators shall have the technical ability to amend or annotate audit log entries for the purpose of correcting data entry errors or system malfunctions, subject to a post-facto notification to the Compliance Department. Such administrator access shall be logged separately, though this secondary log is reviewed only during the annual IT audit.

14.3 Audit trail records shall be preserved in accordance with the retention periods specified in Section 13.

## 15. Exception Handling

15.1 Any deviation from the standard processing workflow described in this Policy shall be recorded in the branch or department exception register, including the reason for the exception and the approving authority.

15.2 Exception registers shall be reviewed by the Compliance Department on a monthly basis as part of routine monitoring.

## 16. Refunds and Failed Transactions

16.1 Where a remittance fails to reach the intended beneficiary due to incorrect account details, incomplete compliance checks, or beneficiary bank rejection, the funds shall be refunded to the originating customer's account, net of any applicable charges, within a reasonable number of business days.

16.2 Refunds arising from Company error shall not attract processing charges.

16.3 All refunds shall require supervisory approval and shall be recorded with supporting reason codes.

## 17. Non-Resident Customers

17.1 Remittance services for Non-Resident Indian ("NRI") and other non-resident customers shall be subject to the same identification and due diligence standards as resident customers, supplemented by verification of overseas address and, where applicable, visa or residency status.

17.2 Where a non-resident customer's KYC was completed by a group entity or correspondent institution in another jurisdiction, ApexFin may rely on such KYC where the group entity is subject to comparable regulatory standards, without independently re-verifying the underlying documents.

## 18. Segregation of Duties

18.1 The functions of transaction initiation, approval, sanctions screening, and reconciliation shall, as far as practicable, be performed by different individuals.

18.2 At smaller branch locations where staffing is limited, a single employee may perform both the initiation and reconciliation functions for a given transaction, provided that Compliance conducts a sample-based post-facto review of such branches on a quarterly basis.

## 19. Management Review

19.1 Branch and departmental heads shall conduct a monthly review of remittance volumes, exceptions, and monitoring alerts pertaining to their area of responsibility.

19.2 A consolidated management review shall be presented to senior management on a quarterly basis.

## 20. Employee Responsibilities

20.1 All employees engaged in remittance operations shall complete mandatory AML and KYC training upon induction and on an annual refresher basis.

20.2 Employees shall promptly report any suspected irregularity, attempted fraud, or policy deviation to their supervisor or the Compliance Department.

## 21. Policy Exceptions

21.1 Any exception to this Policy not otherwise addressed above shall require written approval from the Head of Compliance, documented with business rationale and, where applicable, a time-bound remediation plan.

21.2 Exceptions granted shall be logged centrally and reported to the Board Risk & Compliance Committee at the next scheduled meeting.
